# Skill: Quick Brief

This skill defines how to write a quick brief. Load this file when the report type is `quick-brief`.

---

## Purpose

Answer a specific question clearly and concisely for someone who needs a fast, trustworthy answer. No preamble, no padding, no academic hedging beyond what is necessary. The entire document must fit on one page.

---

## Audience

Anyone who has a specific question and needs a direct, sourced answer quickly. Assumes basic literacy in the subject area but no deep expertise.

---

## Structure

### Question
- Restate the question being answered, exactly as scoped
- One sentence; do not rephrase it to make it broader or narrower than it was asked
- If the question required scoping to be answerable, note the scope in a parenthetical

### Answer
- 1–2 paragraphs
- Lead with the direct answer in the first sentence — do not build to it
- The second paragraph may provide the most essential context or qualification
- If the answer is genuinely uncertain, say so in the first sentence and explain what is known

### Evidence
- 3–5 key sources that support the answer
- For each source:
  - Full citation (author/organization, title, year, URL or DOI if available)
  - 1–2 sentence quote or close paraphrase from the source that supports the answer
- Do not include sources that are tangentially related; every source must directly support a claim in the Answer section

### Caveats
- A bullet list of 1–4 items
- Things the reader should know that qualify the answer: scope limits, recency issues, contested evidence, conditions under which the answer changes
- If there are no meaningful caveats, write "None identified" — do not invent them
- Each caveat is one sentence

### Further Reading
- 2–4 sources for the reader who wants to go deeper
- Each item: citation plus one sentence describing what it adds beyond the answer given here
- Do not repeat sources already listed in the Evidence section

---

## Tone and Style

- Direct: the first word of the Answer section should not be "While", "Although", or "It depends"
- No filler phrases: cut "it is important to note that", "it is worth mentioning", "as we can see"
- Conversational but precise: write for an intelligent adult, not a specialist audience
- Hedge only when the evidence genuinely warrants it; false certainty is worse than honest uncertainty

---

## Length Guidance

- Hard limit: 1 page
- If the answer cannot fit on one page, the question is too broad — note this and scope it down
- Typical length: 300–500 words excluding citations

---

## Format Requirements

- Use `##` for the five section headings (Question, Answer, Evidence, Caveats, Further Reading)
- No subheadings within sections
- Evidence and Further Reading entries are a numbered list
- Caveats are a bullet list
- No tables, no charts — this format is prose only
- Bold may be used once in the Answer section to highlight the core claim, if it aids quick scanning
